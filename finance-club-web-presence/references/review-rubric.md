# Review Rubric

For auditing an existing finance club website, or pressure-testing a plan before building. Score each dimension 0–4, then use the priority guide at the end to sequence fixes.

**Scoring scale**

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Absent |
| 1 | Present but actively damaging (stale, broken, placeholder) |
| 2 | Present and adequate |
| 3 | Good — would hold up next to an established club |
| 4 | A genuine competitive advantage |

---

## 1. Clarity of identity

Can a stranger say what this club is, where it sits, and what it does, within fifteen seconds?

- The hero states name, nature and institution — not just a slogan
- The positioning paragraph is quotable and specific
- The club's distinctive angle is stated and, more importantly, evidenced
- One consistent language throughout; consistent vocabulary for internal structures
- No superlative self-description without something behind it

*A 4 looks like:* the differentiator is not asserted anywhere, because the work on display makes it obvious.

---

## 2. Evidence of work

Is there dated, attributed, downloadable output — and enough of it to show a rhythm?

- At least one recurring publication with a visible cadence
- Every item carries date, named authors, abstract and type
- The archive is reverse-chronological and goes as deep as the club's history allows
- Sourcing and review standards stated once near the archive
- Any pause in a series is acknowledged in plain language
- Portfolio or strategy work, if present, shows mandate, benchmark, methodology and honest results

*A 1 here:* an archive with unexplained gaps, identical boilerplate abstracts, or a metrics table permanently showing zeros.

---

## 3. Organisational legibility

Does the site show that this is an organisation rather than a group of friends?

- Departments or teams each have a remit, activities, deliverables and a named head
- Governance is described briefly (board, how it is chosen, transitions)
- A history timeline exists, however short
- Scale figures are present and each carries a date
- Internal structure named consistently across menu, pages and footer

---

## 4. People

Are there real, accountable, contactable humans?

- Board and members shown with photo, full name, role
- Professional profile links present; direct emails for heads
- Consistent photo treatment across every card — same crop, same style
- Cohort photo labelled with the academic year
- Past boards page exists once there is a past board
- Publications carry bylines that connect to team members

*Consistent photo treatment is worth more than it sounds — mixed crops and backgrounds are the most visible amateurism signal on a club site.*

---

## 5. Audience routing

Can each of the four audiences reach their answer within two clicks of the homepage?

- **Prospective member** → what departments do, what joining involves, current status
- **Sponsor or partner** → what the club offers, audience size, existing partners, a named contact
- **Recruiter or industry** → work with technical depth, alumni destinations
- **Faculty or institution** → legitimacy, governance, public-value work

Check each path literally by clicking. Failures here are usually invisible to the people who built the site.

---

## 6. Freshness

Is anything on the site lying about the present?

- Recruitment page states a current status, not an expired deadline
- Team page and cohort labels match the current academic year
- Footer year is current
- Event pages are not in future tense about past events
- Every statistic is dated
- Latest-work blocks update automatically rather than manually
- No dead social links, no links to nothing

*Anything scoring 1 here should be fixed before anything else on the entire site, regardless of how well other dimensions score. Staleness does more damage than absence.*

---

## 7. Craft and consistency

- One template per content type, applied without exception
- One restrained palette, one or two typefaces, applied to publications as well as pages
- Real photography of the club; no stock filler
- Every navigation item leads somewhere distinct
- No template placeholder copy anywhere
- No duplicated blocks on a page
- Numbered sequences complete and correctly ordered
- Works properly on a phone

---

## 8. Maintainability

Will this site still be true in two years?

- A named role owns the site
- A written rollover checklist exists and is part of the board handover
- No page exists that the club cannot commit to keeping current
- Recurring content pulls from a single source rather than being duplicated by hand
- The publication cadence is one the club can sustain through exam periods

*This dimension predicts every other score two years out. A site scoring 4 everywhere else and 1 here will score 1 everywhere within two cohorts.*

---

## Priority guide

Fix in this order, regardless of relative scores:

1. **Anything actively false** — expired deadlines, wrong team, past events in future tense, placeholder copy, dead links. These cost more credibility than missing sections do.
2. **Maintainability** — assign an owner and write the rollover checklist. Without this, every other fix decays.
3. **Evidence of work** — start or restore the publication cadence. This is the slowest-compounding asset, so it must start earliest.
4. **Audience routing** — cheap to fix, high leverage, usually just navigation and cross-links.
5. **Organisational legibility and people** — mostly writing and photography; achievable in a few weeks.
6. **Craft and consistency** — real but subordinate; a consistent plain site beats an inconsistent elaborate one.
7. **New sections** — partners, alumni, portfolio. Add only once the club can fill them honestly and keep them current.

---

## Quick pre-publish checklist

Before any page goes live:

- [ ] No placeholder or template text remains
- [ ] Every link resolves somewhere distinct and intended
- [ ] Every date and statistic is current and carries an "as of" where relevant
- [ ] Every numbered sequence is complete
- [ ] No block of text appears twice
- [ ] Names, roles and titles are spelled correctly and consistently
- [ ] Someone who did not write the page has read it end to end
- [ ] It has been opened on a phone
